# English Platform for Lessons

A simple interactive educational platform for teaching English to school students.

The platform is designed primarily for Grade 6 students studying English at approximately A2 level. Some B1-level material may be used when appropriate, but lessons must not become unnecessarily difficult or overloaded.

The main goal of the platform is to make English lessons clear, visual, interactive and easy to understand.

---

# 1. Purpose of the Platform

This platform is used during English lessons to:

- explain grammar;
- introduce and revise vocabulary;
- read and discuss texts;
- practise speaking;
- practise listening;
- complete interactive exercises;
- revise previously studied material;
- create topic-based lessons;
- create individual lessons for specific students;
- store teaching materials in one structured place.

The platform should feel like a simple educational environment for children, not like a complicated academic textbook or professional language-learning system.

Content should be easy to read, visually clear and suitable for use during a live lesson.

---

# 2. Target Students

Primary target:

- Grade 6 students;
- approximately 11–13 years old;
- English level: A2;
- occasionally A2+ / early B1.

The platform should not automatically assume that a student understands advanced grammar terminology or complex English explanations.

Material must match the student's actual level.

Do not make a lesson more difficult simply to make it look more serious or educational.

---

# 3. Core Teaching Principle

Always prefer:

**clear → useful → contextual → interactive**

over:

**complex → academic → overloaded → theoretical**

A student should understand:

1. what they are learning;
2. why they are learning it;
3. how the rule works;
4. how to use it;
5. how to practise it.

---

# 4. Language of Explanations

Grammar rules and important explanations must normally be presented in both Russian and English.

Preferred format:

### Русский

Короткое и понятное объяснение правила.

### English

A short and simple English version of the same explanation.

Examples should also normally include a Russian translation.

Example:

> She goes to school every day.  
> Она ходит в школу каждый день.

Do not provide only an English explanation when the rule may be difficult for an A2 student to understand.

Do not provide a long Russian lecture either.

Both versions should be concise and useful.

---

# 5. Grammar Rules

Grammar must follow current standard English usage.

Use modern and accepted English grammar as of the current teaching context.

Do not:

- invent grammar rules;
- simplify a rule until it becomes incorrect;
- use outdated or extremely rare structures without a reason;
- introduce advanced exceptions before the student understands the basic rule;
- use unnecessary linguistic terminology;
- create fake "easy rules" that do not actually work;
- present stylistic preferences as strict grammar rules.

If there is uncertainty about a grammar point:

1. check the approved grammar references in the repository;
2. compare examples;
3. use standard modern English;
4. do not guess;
5. flag the point for review if necessary.

Grammar explanations should normally include:

1. what the structure means;
2. when it is used;
3. the basic form;
4. positive examples;
5. negative examples where relevant;
6. questions where relevant;
7. one or two common mistakes;
8. short practice.

Do not overload an A2 learner with every possible exception.

---

# 6. Vocabulary Rules

Vocabulary should be:

- current;
- useful;
- age-appropriate;
- natural in modern English;
- connected to the lesson topic;
- suitable for approximately A2–B1 level.

Prefer useful phrases and collocations over random isolated words.

For example, prefer:

- `have breakfast`
- `go to school`
- `do homework`

instead of teaching only:

- `breakfast`
- `school`
- `homework`

When appropriate, vocabulary items may include:

- English word or phrase;
- Russian translation;
- example sentence;
- Russian translation of the example;
- pronunciation;
- common collocations.

Do not use obscure vocabulary simply to increase difficulty.

Do not introduce too many new words in one lesson.

---

# 7. Lesson Difficulty

The default lesson level is A2.

B1 material may be introduced only when:

- the student is ready for it;
- it is relevant to the lesson;
- the explanation remains understandable.

Avoid:

- C1/C2 vocabulary;
- advanced academic language;
- overly complex sentence structures;
- long theoretical explanations;
- unnecessary grammar terminology;
- tasks that require knowledge the student has not yet learned.

Difficulty should increase gradually.

---

# 8. Lesson Structure

A lesson should normally move from simple understanding to active use.

Possible lesson blocks include:

1. Warm-up
2. Topic introduction
3. Vocabulary
4. Vocabulary practice
5. Grammar discovery
6. Grammar explanation
7. Controlled grammar practice
8. Reading
9. Listening
10. Speaking
11. Interactive exercise
12. Mini-game
13. Revision
14. Final task
15. Homework

Not every lesson needs every section.

Do not add sections just to make a lesson longer.

Every block must have a clear teaching purpose.

---

# 9. Creating New Lessons

Before creating a lesson:

1. identify the student's level;
2. identify the lesson topic;
3. identify the target grammar;
4. identify the target vocabulary;
5. check what the student has already studied;
6. check relevant repository rules;
7. check approved reference materials where necessary.

A lesson should normally contain:

- one clear main topic;
- one main grammar focus;
- a manageable vocabulary set;
- several different types of practice;
- at least one activity where the student actively produces English.

Whenever possible, reuse previously learned vocabulary and grammar.

Do not create every lesson completely from zero if reusable materials already exist.

---

# 10. Interactive Exercises

Exercises should be simple to understand without long instructions.

Possible formats include:

- multiple choice;
- true / false;
- fill in the gap;
- matching;
- drag and drop;
- sorting;
- sentence building;
- word order;
- flashcards;
- choose the correct form;
- error correction;
- short reading tasks;
- speaking cards;
- mini-games.

Instructions must be short and clear.

Example:

> Choose the correct answer.

is better than:

> Please carefully analyse each of the following sentences and select the option that you believe most accurately completes the grammatical construction.

---

# 11. Exercise Quality

Every automatically checkable exercise must have a clearly correct answer.

Before adding an exercise:

- check that the correct answer is actually correct;
- check that another option cannot also be correct;
- check spelling;
- check grammar;
- check punctuation where relevant;
- check translations;
- check that instructions match the task.

Do not create ambiguous questions.

Do not accidentally reveal the answer in:

- the instruction;
- another example;
- the same exercise;
- a hint;
- surrounding text.

---

# 12. Texts and Reading Materials

Reading texts should match the student's level.

Prefer:

- short paragraphs;
- clear structure;
- familiar vocabulary with a small number of new words;
- topics relevant to children and teenagers;
- natural modern English.

Avoid unnecessarily long texts.

Do not create artificial or unnatural English only to demonstrate a grammar rule.

A text should sound like real English.

---

# 13. Writing Style

All student-facing content must be clean and natural.

Avoid:

- AI-style filler;
- unnecessary introductions;
- corporate language;
- vague motivational phrases;
- excessive headings;
- repetitive explanations;
- strange metaphors;
- meaningless text;
- placeholder text;
- gibberish;
- broken encoding;
- random symbols;
- unfinished sentences.

Never display internal AI instructions to students.

Never display technical implementation notes inside a lesson.

---

# 14. Russian Text Quality

Russian explanations and translations must sound natural.

Do not produce literal machine translations.

Avoid unnatural constructions such as translations that copy English word order when normal Russian would use a different structure.

The Russian version should explain the meaning, not mechanically reproduce every word.

---

# 15. English Text Quality

Use natural modern English.

Check:

- spelling;
- grammar;
- articles;
- prepositions;
- collocations;
- word order;
- punctuation;
- tense consistency.

Do not invent expressions that native speakers would not normally use.

When British and American English differ, use one consistent version inside the same lesson unless the difference itself is being taught.

---

# 16. Reference Materials

Repository reference materials may include:

- school textbooks;
- workbooks;
- grammar books;
- vocabulary references;
- teacher notes;
- curriculum notes.

Use these materials as references.

Do not assume that every textbook explanation is appropriate to copy directly into a lesson.

Adapt explanations to the student's level.

Do not reproduce large copyrighted sections of textbooks verbatim.

Create original explanations and original exercises based on the teaching objective.

---

# 17. Educational Accuracy

Never invent:

- grammar rules;
- word meanings;
- translations;
- pronunciations;
- example usage;
- cultural facts;
- historical facts;
- statistics.

If reliable information is unavailable, do not fill the gap with a guess.

Use approved references or request clarification.

Educational correctness is more important than generating content quickly.

---

# 18. Design Principle

The platform should remain:

- simple;
- readable;
- modern;
- child-friendly;
- visually structured;
- interactive without being distracting.

Avoid:

- visual overload;
- excessive animation;
- too many colours;
- tiny text;
- giant text blocks;
- unnecessary decorative elements;
- complicated navigation;
- interfaces that resemble administrative dashboards.

The lesson itself must remain the main focus.

Detailed visual rules are stored separately in:

`rules/DESIGN.md`

---

# 19. Content Separation

Keep educational content separate from interface logic whenever possible.

Do not hardcode large amounts of lesson content directly into interface components if the project structure provides a separate place for lessons or content.

Prefer reusable components.

For example:

- one reusable multiple-choice component;
- one reusable flashcard component;
- one reusable grammar card;
- one reusable speaking card.

Do not build a completely new visual system for every lesson.

---

# 20. Existing Content

Do not modify unrelated lessons when creating or editing one lesson.

Do not delete existing material unless explicitly requested.

Do not rename or reorganise major project folders without a clear reason.

Before making a large structural change:

1. inspect the existing project;
2. understand how the current structure works;
3. preserve compatibility where possible.

---

# 21. Common Errors to Avoid

### Error: lesson is too difficult

Solution:

- reduce vocabulary;
- shorten sentences;
- remove unnecessary terminology;
- split the grammar point into smaller steps.

### Error: explanation is too long

Solution:

- keep only information required for the current task;
- move secondary details to an optional note.

### Error: exercise has two possible answers

Solution:

- rewrite the sentence;
- add necessary context;
- replace ambiguous answer options.

### Error: grammar rule is oversimplified and becomes incorrect

Solution:

- keep the basic explanation simple but accurate;
- add a short note instead of creating a false rule.

### Error: vocabulary sounds unnatural

Solution:

- check common modern usage;
- prefer common collocations and phrases.

### Error: Russian translation sounds mechanical

Solution:

- translate the meaning naturally;
- do not copy English word order.

### Error: lesson contains random visual styles

Solution:

- reuse existing components;
- follow `rules/DESIGN.md`.

### Error: a new feature breaks an old lesson

Solution:

- avoid unnecessary global changes;
- test existing components after shared code is modified.

### Error: generated content contains strange symbols or broken text

Solution:

- check text encoding;
- use UTF-8;
- remove placeholder or corrupted characters before publishing.

# 22. Before Publishing or Finishing a Lesson

Check:

- [ ] Is the level appropriate?
- [ ] Is the grammar correct?
- [ ] Is the vocabulary natural?
- [ ] Are Russian translations natural?
- [ ] Are explanations short enough?
- [ ] Are difficult rules explained in Russian and English?
- [ ] Are examples translated where appropriate?
- [ ] Are exercise answers unambiguous?
- [ ] Is there any accidental answer leakage?
- [ ] Are instructions clear?
- [ ] Is the design consistent?
- [ ] Does the lesson work on the page?
- [ ] Are there broken buttons or interactions?
- [ ] Is any placeholder text still visible?
- [ ] Did the change affect unrelated lessons?

# 24. Main Principle

This platform is made for students, not for developers and not for AI.

Every decision should answer the question:

**Will this make the lesson clearer and more useful for the student?**

If the answer is no, the element probably does not need to be added.
